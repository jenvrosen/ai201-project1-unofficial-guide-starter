# How To Follow a Healthy Endometriosis Diet and Why

## What is an endometriosis diet?

An endometriosis diet focuses on reducing inflammation associated with the condition. According to Dr. Cara King, "Endometriosis is exceptionally inflammatory. Anything that's going to decrease that [inflammation] is going to be helpful."

However, dietary changes represent just one component of comprehensive endometriosis management. Dr. King emphasizes that "Diet can help. But proper treatment and management go beyond what you eat."

## Best and worst foods for endometriosis

### Eat more: Fiber

High estrogen levels worsen endometriosis symptoms. Fiber helps the body eliminate excess estrogen through bowel movements. Aim for approximately 35 grams daily. Dr. King notes, "If you're pooping once a day, you're probably on track."

Fiber-rich foods include:
- Fruits and vegetables (whole foods, not juice)
- Ground flaxseed
- Legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas)
- Whole grains (whole-wheat pasta, brown rice)

Increase fiber gradually to minimize bloating and gas. Drink plenty of water.

### Eat more: Healthy fats

Omega-3 fatty acids reduce inflammation. Good sources include:
- Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, tuna)
- Nuts and seeds (walnuts, chia seeds, flaxseed)
- Plant oils (flaxseed oil, canola oil)

Monounsaturated fats offer anti-inflammatory benefits and appear in avocados, nuts, seeds, olive oil, peanut butter, and safflower oil.

### Eat more: Magnesium

Magnesium eases muscle cramps, including menstrual cramps. Sources include:
- Fruits (bananas, papaya, avocados, blackberries)
- Leafy greens (arugula, lettuce, kale, spinach)
- Legumes (black beans, edamame)
- Nuts and seeds (almonds, pumpkin seeds)

### Eat more: Zinc

Zinc regulates menstrual cycles and supports hormonal balance by promoting ovulation and progesterone production, which counters excess estrogen.

High-zinc foods include:
- Poultry (chicken, turkey)
- Red meat (limit to two low-fat servings weekly)
- Shellfish (oysters, crab, lobster)
- Eggs (limit to one daily)

Vegetarian and vegan individuals should consult healthcare professionals about zinc sources and supplementation.

### Limit: Highly processed foods

Processed foods contain minimal vitamins and minerals while featuring inflammation-promoting ingredients:
- Simple carbohydrates
- Sugar
- Salt
- Saturated fats
- Preservatives
- Food dyes

These foods can worsen endometriosis symptoms while displacing nutrient-dense options.

### Cut out: Food triggers

Individual food sensitivities vary considerably. Dr. King recommends maintaining a "food journal so you and your provider can identify any triggering foods. Note what you're eating and any symptoms you're experiencing."

Common triggers include:
- Alcohol
- Caffeine
- Gluten
- Dairy products
- High-sugar foods

## Bottom line

Diet did not cause endometriosis and cannot cure it independently. However, healthy eating supports overall wellness. Dr. King emphasizes that "Eating well can go a long way toward helping you keep your body healthy overall, and that can help you feel your best."

Comprehensive management requires discussing symptom management strategies, mental health support, and overall well-being with healthcare providers.
